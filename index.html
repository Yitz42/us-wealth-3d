How the numbers are built

Share of wealth
WID's share of total net personal wealth held by each 1-percentile group. Bins with more debt than assets are negative (shown in red).
Nominal dollars per household
share × total household net worth (Fed Z.1, year-end) ÷ (households ÷ 100). This gives the average net worth of a household in that 1% group, in dollars of that year.
Real dollars, CPI-U / PCE
Years of consumer spending
Average net worth in the group ÷ consumer spending per household (PCE ÷ households). A value of 10 means the group's wealth would pay for 10 years of the average household's spending.
Adults vs households
WID ranks adults aged 20+, with each couple's wealth split equally between them, not households. It is the only source with 1-percentile detail back to 1950, so its shares are applied to household totals here. Fed data, which ranks households, puts the top 1% about 5 points lower than WID does.
Grey pillars: estimated values
WID publishes each group's share rounded to 0.01% of all wealth. Groups holding almost nothing, or owing almost as much as they own, come out as exactly 0.00%, which hides both their size and whether they are in debt. Each run of these zeros is estimated by drawing a straight line between the nearest published groups on either side; wealth always rises with rank, so the line stays in order. A run at the very bottom (in the 1950s) is extended from the trend of the next 20 groups. Every estimate stays within ±0.0045%, so it still rounds to WID's 0.00%.
Income
The Income measure is WID's pre-tax national income for each 1% group, 1950–2024: wages plus business and investment income, before taxes and transfers, with pensions counted when they are paid out. It is broader than salary alone. Dollar views apply these shares to US national income (BEA) per household, the same way wealth shares are applied to household net worth. Groups WID rounds to exactly 0.00% are estimated in grey, as for wealth. There is no 1% income data after 2024 yet.
